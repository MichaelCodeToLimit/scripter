Evidence (what the judge was shown; for an image, a description of what was sent)
**Verdict:** ❌ won't work as designed. 2x4s laid flat at 600 mm o.c. over a 2.4 m span fail both the strength and deflection check by a wide margin — and even flipped on edge at that spacing they're only marginal. The 12 mm ply is also too thin for a 600 mm joist spacing.

**Understanding:** You want a walkable garage storage loft, 2.4 m clear span, 2x4 joists @ 600 mm o.c., 12 mm ply deck. Key assumption: design load ≈ 2.0 kN/m² (1.9 kN/m² live for boxes + occasional foot traffic, standard residential floor allowance, + ~0.15 kN/m² dead load) — *assumed*, flag if you're planning to store something dense (engine blocks, tile, etc.).

**How it works:** A joist resists bending mainly through its *depth*, not its width — bending stiffness scales with depth³. "Laid flat" puts the 38 mm dimension vertical, so you're using the weak axis: about **5.5× less stiff** and **2.3× less strong** than the same 2x4 on edge. The load path also has to run joist → something solid at the wall (ledger/hanger into studs, not just drywall) → wall framing → slab. Ply deck spans between joists and needs its own span rating independent of joist strength.

**Numbers** (per joist, 600 mm tributary width, simply supported, UDL):
- Load per joist: 2.0 kN/m² × 0.6 m = 1.2 kN/m *(estimated)*
- Bending moment: wL²/8 = 1.2 × 2.4²/8 = 0.86 kN·m
- Required section modulus (Fb ≈ 7.5 MPa, typical SPF No.2, *estimated*): S = M/Fb ≈ 115,000 mm³
  - 2x4 **flat** (b=89, d=38): S = 21,400 mm³ → **18% of what's needed**
  - 2x4 **on edge** (b=38, d=89): S = 50,100 mm³ → **43% of what's needed**
- Deflection (limit L/360 = 6.7 mm), E ≈ 9,500 MPa *(estimated)*:
  - 2x4 flat: ≈ 134 mm sag (20× the limit)
  - 2x4 on edge: ≈ 24 mm sag (3.6× the limit)
- 12 mm ply: typical rated span for foot-traffic sheathing is ~400–480 mm o.c.; 600 mm is beyond that regardless of joist size.

**Problems (worst first):**
1. Flat orientation — wrong axis for spanning; this alone makes it unsafe to stand on.
2. Even corrected to on-edge, a 2x4 at 2.4 m / 600 mm o.c. still fails deflection (and is close on strength) — undersized member.
3. 12 mm ply at 600 mm spacing will flex/feel spongy underfoot and is under typical span ratings.
4. Not stated: what the joist ends actually bear on. If it's toe-nailed into drywall or resting unsecured on a stud face, the load path is incomplete regardless of joist size.

**Better option:** Standard DIY garage loft practice is 2x6 or 2x8 on edge at 400–600 mm o.c. with ≥18 mm (3/4") ply, on a ledger lag-bolted into every wall stud (or hung with joist hangers) plus bearing on the top plate. That matches the numbers below.

**Improvements (ranked):**
1. **Orient joists on edge**, not flat. Non-negotiable — fixes the weak-axis problem. Trade-off: none, same material cost.
2. **Upsize joists.** Two ways to hit the numbers:
   - Keep 600 mm o.c. → use **2x8 (38×184 mm)** on edge. Check: S = 214,000 mm³ (1.9× margin), deflection ≈ 2.8 mm (well under 6.7 mm limit). 
   - Keep smaller joists → **2x6 (38×140 mm)** on edge at **400 mm o.c.** instead of 600. Trade-off: more joists, more labor, but shallower (less headroom loss) and cheaper lumber.
3. **Match the decking to spacing.** If you go with 600 mm o.c., upgrade to 18 mm (3/4") ply/OSB — 12 mm isn't rated for that span under foot traffic. If you go 400 mm o.c., 12 mm ply is fine.
4. **Confirm joist-end support.** Ledger board lag-bolted into wall studs (not just the sheathing), with joists resting on it via joist hangers or a doubled top plate — not toe-nailed alone.
5. **Add blocking** between joists at mid-span — stops rotation/buckling and stiffens deck edges under point loads (feet, box corners).

**Questions:**
1. What will the joist ends actually bear on — ledger into studs, hangers, top plate? *Default: ledger lag-bolted into every stud + joist hangers.*
2. Storage weight — general boxes, or anything dense (tools, tile, car parts)? *Default: general storage, 1.9 kN/m² live load covers it.*
3. Keep 600 mm spacing (2x8) or go tighter at 400 mm (2x6)? *Default: 2x8 @ 600 mm, since you specified that spacing — possibly to match existing roof framing.*

**Next:** Once you confirm the spacing/size option and end-support detail, I'll give the full build plan — cut list, ledger bolt pattern and spacing, joist hanger/fastener schedule, blocking layout, and build sequence. Also worth a quick local building-code check and, since this is load-bearing and walked on, a sanity check by someone qualified if you're at all unsure about the wall framing it'll hang from.
