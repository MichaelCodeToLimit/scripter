A new design, a review or a build request gets a short report before anything is made. It always starts with the verdict.

## The verdict

<VerdictScale compact />

The verdict follows from the numbers. Scripter is told never to decide it first and then argue for it.

## The sections

Sections always come in this order. Any that would be empty are dropped.

| Section | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | One of the four above, plus one line saying why. |
| **Understanding** | 1–3 lines: the goal, and the key assumptions, each marked *assumed*. Check these first; a wrong assumption is the cheapest thing to fix. |
| **How it works** | The working model: how this kind of thing works, in a few lines. |
| **Numbers** | For each quantity that decides the outcome (power, force, heat, water, data, cost, time): what's available and what's needed, with units. Each figure is marked *known*, *estimated* or *assumed*. |
| **Problems** | Numbered, worst first. |
| **Better option** | Only when a better approach exists. If yours is better than the standard solution, it keeps yours and says why. |
| **Improvements** | Ranked by impact. Each says what to change, why, and the trade-off. |
| **Questions** | Only questions whose answer would change the design. Each has a default. |
| **Next** | What it will build once you confirm. |

## An example

For the prompt *"A drone with a solar panel on top so it charges while flying and never has to land"*:

```text
**Verdict:** ❌ won't work as designed. A panel that fits on the drone makes about a tenth of the power it needs to hover.
**How it works:** a multirotor holds itself up by pushing air down all the time; hover power rises with weight.
**Numbers:** available ≈ 0.1 m² × 1000 W/m² × 20% ≈ 20 W at noon, clear sky (estimated) · needed ≈ 150–200 W to hover at 1 kg (estimated, typical quadcopter).
**Problems (worst first):** 1. Power shortfall of about 10×. 2. A bigger panel adds weight and drag, which raises the power it needs.
**Better option:** perch and charge. It lands on a high point, charges from the panel, then flies again. Or use a fixed-wing solar glider, which needs several times less power per kg.
**Questions:** what's the mission, for example watching one area all day? Default: stationary surveillance.
**Next:** once you pick a direction, I'll size the panel, the battery and the duty cycle.
```

## How to read the numbers

- **"Needed" means the hardest point of the cycle, including losses.** Friction, drag, leakage and efficiency count. The energy to *reach* a speed is not the power to *hold* it there. This is the most common way a design looks feasible on paper and fails in use.
- **Totals are over the whole cycle.** For example, litres per wash or kWh per day, not just the first step.
- **Figures are compared with real products.** If an estimate comes out 10× lower than what existing products use, Scripter is told to find out why before trusting it.
- **The figures are rough.** They're for deciding whether an idea is worth pursuing, not for final engineering.

## Replying

- **"OK"** confirms the verdict and the assumptions, and it builds what's under **Next**.
- **Change an assumption** ("it's for a 2 kg drone", "I have a laser cutter") and it re-checks before building.
- **Disagree** and it re-checks from first principles. See [When you disagree](/scripter/guides/pushing-back/).
