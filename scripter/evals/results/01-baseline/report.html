This design won't work, so I want to flag that before making anything: a ring of magnets around a wheel can't produce net rotational force over a full turn. Every magnet that gets pulled toward the next one also has to be pulled away from the one it just passed — those forces are equal, so they cancel over a complete revolution. This is a direct consequence of conservation of energy: magnets are static fields with no energy source, so they can't add net energy to the system turn after turn. It's the same reason perpetual motion magnet motors have never worked despite over a century of attempts (there's no patent for one, and patent offices generally reject them outright for this reason).

Any real prototype will settle at an equilibrium point (or oscillate briefly) and stop — it won't spin continuously, and definitely won't output more energy than friction/electrical load consumes.

What I can actually do for you:

1. **Build the 3D model anyway**, purely as a visualization of the concept (e.g., in Blender/OpenSCAD/Three.js) — useful for a presentation, a thought experiment, or to see visually *why* it stalls.
2. **Model it plus simulate the forces** so you can see the net torque come out to zero over a rotation — more convincing than just a static model.
3. **Point you at energy sources that actually work** with a generator — wind, water flow, a hand crank, a small solar panel, a temperature differential (Stirling engine) — any of which I could help you design/model instead.

# Water-Turbine-Driven Washing Machine (No Motor)

## Core idea
Mains water pressure is the only power source. The trick that makes it viable: **route the turbine's spent water into the tub as fill/rinse water**, so the "fuel" for spinning the drum isn't wasted — it does mechanical work first, then becomes wash water. Only during spin/extraction does turbine exhaust get diverted straight to drain (since you don't want to keep adding water to a load you're trying to dry).

## Power budget (why this is actually feasible)
- Typical mains supply: ~3 bar (300 kPa), ~15 L/min available flow.
- Hydraulic power = ΔP × Q ≈ 300,000 Pa × 2.5×10⁻⁴ m³/s ≈ **75 W** available.
- A small Pelton-style turbine at ~60% efficiency → **~45 W mechanical** at the shaft.
- Wash/tumble load: rotating a drum at ~45 rpm needs only tens of watts — easily covered continuously.
- Spin/extraction: spinning a ~0.15 kg·m² drum+load up to 800 rpm needs only ~500 J of kinetic energy — at 45 W that's roughly 1-2 minutes of pure spin-up energy, but real friction/windage/water-drag losses will stretch this to **5-10 minutes** for full extraction speed (vs. ~1 min for a motorized machine). Slower, but genuinely achievable — not a case of "not enough power," just less power than a 300-500 W spin motor.

## Turbine
- **Pelton wheel**, not a propeller/axial turbine — it suits the high-head/low-flow regime of a household tap and is simple to make reversible with a double bucket row.
- Fed by a fixed nozzle off the inlet solenoid-free valve; nozzle diameter sized to the ~3 bar / 15 L/min point for best efficiency.
- Sealed housing with a shaft seal (or magnetic coupling, to eliminate seal wear entirely) driving into the gearbox.

## Drivetrain
- **Two-stage reduction gearbox** with a mechanical clutch/dog-engagement, not unlike a bicycle's two gears:
  - **Wash gear**: high reduction (~20:1) → low rpm, high torque for gentle tumbling.
  - **Spin gear**: near-direct drive (low reduction) → lets turbine rpm translate to drum rpm for extraction.
- Engagement between the two is done by a cam-actuated dog clutch, itself driven off the sequencing camshaft (see below) — no solenoids needed.

## Reversing tumble action
- Pelton runner has **buckets on both faces**, each face fed by its own nozzle.
- A small rotary spool valve (piloted mechanically, not electrically) alternates flow between the two nozzle sets every ~10-15 seconds, reversing drum direction — mimicking standard front-loader wash agitation.

## Cycle sequencing — all mechanical
- Tap a **worm gear off the main turbine shaft** to drive a slow camshaft (~1 rev per several minutes).
- Cam lobes operate small pilot/diaphragm valves in sequence: fill → wash (with periodic reversal) → drain → rinse → spin → drain.
- This is essentially a hydro-mechanical version of the old electromechanical washer timer — a "water computer," no electronics required.

## Extending agitation without wasting water
- Since continuous tumbling requires continuous flow, and you don't want fresh water running the entire soak: charge a small **hydraulic accumulator (bladder tank)** during the fill phase.
- After fill, valve closes to mains; the accumulator bleeds its stored pressure through the turbine in metered pulses, giving several more minutes of tumbling from water you already have in the tub — not new draw.
- Real washers already have "soak" pauses with no motion, so this matches expected wash behavior.

## Drain
- If your standpipe is below tub level (typical residential setup): plain gravity + a cam-timed drain valve, no pump needed at all.
- If drain must go uphill: a **venturi eductor** off the mains supply (same principle as bilge aspirators) creates suction to lift wastewater — no motor, just a bit of extra mains water as motive fluid.

## Detergent dispensing
- A venturi/eductor tapped off the fill line drafts detergent from a reservoir automatically during fill — same trick used in pressure-washer soap injectors, fully passive.

## Water consumption tradeoff (be upfront about this)
- Wash/rinse phases: no penalty — turbine water becomes wash water.
- Spin phase: pure consumption, since turbine exhaust must go to drain rather than into the tub. Expect an extra ~15-20 L per spin cycle purely as motive water (comparable to an extra rinse in a conventional machine).
- Mitigation: since this water never touches the laundry, it's clean — pipe it to a greywater catch tank for garden/toilet reuse instead of straight to drain.

## Safety essentials
- Backflow preventer / air gap between mains and tub water (cross-connection control — required by plumbing code anyway).
- Water hammer arrestor near the reversing valve (rapid flow direction changes will bang without one).
- Pressure-relief on the accumulator.

## Honest limitations vs. a motorized machine
- Spin cycle takes noticeably longer (single-digit minutes, not one).
- Performance is coupled to your actual mains pressure/flow — low-pressure or restricted-flow properties (well pumps, apartments with weak pressure) will underperform badly; may need a pressure/flow rating printed on the machine like an appliance spec.
- Slightly higher water use overall (mainly from the spin phase), offset if greywater is recaptured.
- No fine electronic control (fuzzy load sensing, precise temperature) — this is a mechanically simple, robust, off-grid-friendly machine, not a feature-rich one.

This is a legitimate, buildable concept — closest real-world analogues are 19th/20th-century water-motor-driven mangles and laundry equipment, and modern off-grid/appropriate-technology water-powered tools (e.g., hydraulic ram pumps, water-driven grain mills) that use the same "pressure differential as the only energy source" principle.
