# Buildings, rooms and structures

Checklists and rough rules for the working model (step 2) and the feasibility check (step 4). Every value is **rough** and codes differ by country. **Always flag that local building rules apply**, and recommend a structural engineer for anything that carries people or a building.

## Load path

- Trace every load continuously down to the ground: roof → rafters → walls or beams → columns → foundations → soil. A gap anywhere in the chain means it fails.
- Loads to consider: its own weight, people and contents, snow, wind (including uplift), and point loads such as a bath or a bookcase.
- Rough residential floor load: about 1.5–2 kN/m² of people and contents, on top of the floor's own weight.
- Openings in load-bearing walls need a beam (lintel) and something solid under each end.
- Anything that sways needs bracing: diagonals, shear panels or rigid joints.

## Rough member sizes (for sanity checks only)

| Member | Rule of thumb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Timber floor joist (at ~400 mm spacing) | depth ≈ span / 20–24 | 4 m span → about 175–200 mm deep |
| Steel beam | depth ≈ span / 20 | 6 m span → about 300 mm deep |
| Concrete slab | thickness ≈ span / 30 | 5 m span → about 170 mm |
| Shelf (wood, 18 mm) | span up to about 70–80 cm for books | longer spans sag |

## Circulation and clearances

- Doors: clear width at least about 80 cm, and about 85–90 cm for wheelchairs.
- Corridors: at least about 90 cm, and about 120 cm for wheelchairs.
- Stairs: rise about 15–19 cm and going about 25–30 cm (2 × rise + going ≈ 60–63 cm). Headroom at least about 2.0 m. Handrail at about 90 cm.
- Ceiling height: about 2.4 m is typical for living spaces.
- Space to use things: about 1 m in front of kitchen counters and wardrobes, and room to open every door and drawer.

## Daylight and ventilation

- Glazing of about 10–20% of the floor area for daylight.
- Every room needs fresh air. Kitchens and bathrooms need extraction.

## Services

- Water supply, and drainage that falls continuously (about 1:40 for small waste pipes), with traps and venting.
- Electrical: circuits and their capacity, and the zones where water is present.
- Heating, cooling and ventilation routes, and space for the plant.

## Moisture, insulation and condensation

- Keep rain out, let vapour escape, and put the vapour barrier on the warm side of the insulation.
- Cold bridges (steel, concrete through the insulation) cause condensation and mould.
- Wood rots if it stays wet. Keep it off the ground and ventilated.

## Fire

- Escape routes from every room, with limited travel distance and no route that passes through a room at higher risk.
- Smoke alarms, and fire separation between homes and garages.

## Accessibility

- Step-free entry, door and corridor widths, turning space (about 1.5 m circle), and switch heights.

## Codes and permits

Rules and permits vary by country and region: planning permission, building regulations, and heritage or zoning rules. Always name this as an open item and point the user to their local rules or authority.
